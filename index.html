How the verdicts are worked out

Confidence checklist is the headline for each coin: twelve pass or fail checks. Ten are safety checks: liquidity of $50k or more, liquidity at 3% or more of market cap, a pair more than a day old, a website or socials, two-way trading (50 or more trades in the hour with sells at least a quarter of buys), mint and freeze authority revoked, 90% of the liquidity pool locked or burned, top 10 holders under 30%, developer under 5%, and no insider cluster over 10%. The last two are momentum at the buy line and the chart agreeing. Checks that need data the page could not get are shown as not checked and do not count as passes.

Signal score runs from −100 to +100 and now leans on trading flow more than price: price change over 5 minutes (up to ±10), 1 hour (±24) and 6 hours (±16); buy pressure, the share of the last hour's trades that were buys (±45, needs 20 or more trades); sustained buying over 6 hours (±20, needs 50 or more trades); volume trend against the 6-hour average (−15 to +20); and a −20 penalty when a coin is already up more than 300% in 24 hours.

Buy needs a score of +35 or more, low rug risk, and no more than one failed safety check. Sell is a score of −30 or less, or one of your own exit rules firing: 25% below the entry you marked, or 35% off the peak after being up 30% or more. Hold is everything between. Rug risk overrides the rest when the rug score reaches 60. Avoid means under $10k of liquidity.

Confidence on a buy is strong at 10 or more checks passed, moderate at 8 or 9, and weak below that. It is capped at moderate when the on-chain checks could not be loaded.

Coverage is about 150 to 200 Solana coins at a time, not every coin on the chain: your watchlist, DexScreener's boosted and recently updated tokens, GeckoTerminal's trending pools, the highest-volume pools of the last 24 hours, and the newest pairs. Stablecoins, SOL and staking tokens are left out. On-chain checks are loaded for coins near a buy, coins you hold or watch, and any coin you open; the rest show them as not checked.

Background tracker is a scheduled job in the GitHub repository that runs the same scoring about every 90 seconds, day and night, and saves every buy signal and its exit. It follows the trending scan, new pairs and the coins in the repository's watchlist file, which is separate from the watchlist in this browser. It has no chart data, so its checklists show the chart check as not checked.

Auto wallets are four simulated wallets run by the background tracker, so they trade whether or not this page is open. Each starts with 10 SOL and follows its own fixed rule set, which is written out in full at the top of its tab. Fills include estimated price impact and 1% costs each way, and a coin that can no longer be priced is written off as a total loss. They check about every 90 seconds, so they react later than a person watching live would.

Rule review, 12 October 2026. After the first day all three wallets were losing, and the recorded data showed where: coins bought after they had already risen a long way, thinly traded coins, and losers held down to a 25% stop. The wallets were restarted from 10 SOL on new rules. All three now skip coins already up more than 50% in 24 hours, stop out at 12% down, and sell when the momentum score fades instead of waiting for a Sell verdict. "Quick profit" sells everything at 8% up. "Established coins" only buys pools with at least $250k of liquidity that have not moved more than 15% in the hour, and lets winners run on a 10% trailing stop. "Adaptive" starts close to that and is adjusted by the daily tuning. A fourth wallet, "Recovery", was added the same day to test the one signal that was ahead in the first day's data: it follows the Quick profit rules but only buys coins that are lower than 24 hours ago. Replayed over that first day these rules lost about 1% where the old ones lost 3% to 24%, but that is the same data they were chosen on, so it flatters them.

Adaptive wallet and daily tuning. Once a day the tracker replays up to 14 days of its recorded snapshots through the adaptive wallet's current rules and through every one-step variation of them: a different buy line, liquidity floor, stop-loss, trailing stop and so on. A variation is adopted only when it beats the current rules on the older part of the data and also on the most recent part, which it was not chosen on, by at least half a percentage point and with enough trades. Nothing changes until there are 5 days of data and 50 replayed trades, and at most one rule changes per day. The original and tighter wallets never change, so they act as benchmarks. Tuning adjusts thresholds only; it cannot invent new signals, and rules that fit past data can still fail on new data.

Signal research runs once a day in the background tracker. It takes a list of candidate buy rules, such as "down on the day and now being bought", "holder count growing" or "more distinct buyers than sellers", finds every time each one became true in the recorded snapshots, and measures what the price did over the next one to two hours. Results are split into an older and a recent period, and a signal has to be ahead in both to be called promising. It is a way of finding leads to test in a wallet. It does not change any rules by itself.

Demo wallet uses play money and never touches a real wallet. Balances, trade amounts and profit or loss are in SOL, so results reflect what a SOL holder would end up with; token prices stay in dollars to match the charts. A demo buy fills above the quoted price by the estimated price impact for that size, and each trade pays 1% in costs. Holdings are valued at what a sale would return right now after impact and costs, so a position starts slightly down. Real trading can still do worse: failed transactions, front-running and tokens that tax or block sales. A demo buy also marks your entry, so the stop-loss and trailing-stop rules apply to it. It is saved in this browser only.

Track record logs each buy signal given while this page is open, once per coin per 24 hours, then looks up the price 1, 6 and 24 hours later from chart history. It is stored in this browser only. Until it holds 30 or more measured signals the averages mean very little.

Rug score adds points for each warning sign: very low or draining liquidity, liquidity that is tiny next to market cap, a pair only hours old, a price collapse, buys with almost no sells (a honeypot pattern), volume far above liquidity, no website or socials, and heavy paid promotion on a new pair. When RugCheck responds, its on-chain findings are added, along with holder concentration, developer holdings and insider clusters. 60 or more is High, 30 to 59 is Elevated.

Chart read uses the candles on the timeframe you pick: RSI over 14 candles, the 9 and 21 candle moving averages, the volume-weighted average price, recent volume against earlier volume, and where the price sits between the low (support) and high (resistance) of the previous 20 candles. It is shown beside the verdict as a second opinion and does not change the score.

Suggested stop and target put the stop just under support when that is 3% to 30% away, otherwise two typical candle ranges (ATR) below the price. The target is resistance when that is at least 5% up, otherwise twice the distance to the stop. The trade planner sizes a position so that hitting the stop loses the amount you chose.

Recent trades covers the latest trades the data source returns for the main pool, up to 300. Large trades are those over $500 or 0.5% of liquidity, whichever is bigger.

Prices, liquidity and trade counts come from DexScreener. Candles, individual trades and the new pairs list come from GeckoTerminal. On-chain checks come from RugCheck.

Price impact is an estimate from pooled liquidity of how far a buy of that size would move the price. Real slippage is usually worse.

These are fixed rules applied to public market data and have not been backtested. They cannot see insider wallets, coordinated dumps or news, and a low rug score does not make a coin safe. Treat this as a screening aid and not as financial advice. Most meme coins go to zero.

Signal outcomes follows every buy signal from the price when it was given until an exit: the verdict turning to Sell, Rug risk or Avoid, a 25% fall from the signal price, or a 35% fall from the peak after being up 30%. Profit or loss is price to price, before trading costs. Signals and exits are only seen while the page is open, so an exit after a gap is marked late and may be worse than a live exit would have been.
