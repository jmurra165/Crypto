How the verdicts are worked out

Signal score runs from −100 to +100 and is the sum of six parts: price change over 5 minutes (up to ±15), 1 hour (±36) and 6 hours (±20); buy pressure, meaning the share of the last hour's trades that were buys (±60, needs 20 or more trades); volume trend, meaning the last hour's volume against the 6-hour average (−15 to +20); and a −20 penalty when a coin is already up more than 300% in 24 hours. Open any row to see each part's contribution.

Buy needs a score of +35 or more and low rug risk. Sell is a score of −30 or less, or one of your own exit rules firing: 25% below the entry you marked, or 35% off the peak after being up 30% or more. Hold is everything between, including strong momentum held back by elevated rug risk. Rug risk overrides the rest when the rug score reaches 60. Avoid means under $10k of liquidity.

Confidence on a buy or sell counts how many timeframes agree, whether there are 100 or more trades in the hour, and whether liquidity is above $100k.

Rug score adds points for each warning sign: very low or draining liquidity, liquidity that is tiny next to market cap, a pair only hours old, a price collapse, buys with almost no sells (a honeypot pattern), volume far above liquidity, no website or socials, and heavy paid promotion on a new pair. When RugCheck responds, its on-chain findings are added. 60 or more is High, 30 to 59 is Elevated.

Price impact is an estimate from pooled liquidity of how far a buy of that size would move the price. Real slippage is usually worse.

These are fixed rules applied to public market data and have not been backtested. They cannot see insider wallets, coordinated dumps or news, and a low rug score does not make a coin safe. Treat this as a screening aid and not as financial advice. Most meme coins go to zero.
